Last night I took my two older kids to see Primetime, the Chris Hansen exposé (of sorts). Lucy and I had similar takes. The vintage 90s vaporwave/VHS cinematography was a bold choice. That combined with the choppy editing and nonstop movement really amped up the intensity. I kept thinking to myself I've never seen a movie quite like this. It felt to me like I was really seeing something new, which I appreciated. Excellent job by Pattinson, as expected. There were a few fantastic lines, most of which unfortunately you get in the trailers. But the one scene where Dan is talking to the whale and Pattinson does a slow finger gun motion to his crotch had me cracking up. When they got to the town of Murphy, Texas, everybody's mind was kind of blown. All that stuff is just like 5 mins down the road from where we saw the movie. Alex Winter had the look of a Texas pol down so hard it was unbelievable. Small part but had a lot of impact. The Murphy police chief kind of ran away with his scenes. The "disgusting Democrat!" line had us cracking up. Gonna have to look that guy up because I'm guessing I've seen him since running for local office. There have been a few prominent black Republicans here over the years.

( 🗒️ *I looked it up after I originally wrote this, and the Murphy cop in real life was a white guy. I never saw him after those events. He was sued into obscurity, and the charges against 23 pedos Hansen had rounded up in addition to the assistant DA who committed suicide were all DROPPED by the head DA. Now how's that for perverted justice?* )

My own take was I went in expecting a critique of Chris Hansen as a man, as a journalist, and I felt like the movie defied my expectations a bit. About 2/3 of the way through it sort of pivoted and Hansen became a man pressured by his own secrets while simultaneously on a Moby Dick-style crusade of metaphysical dimensions. The demon shown in the film may have been a metaphor, but it was played quite literally. And Hansen wasn't swallowed up by his own ambition; in fact, he sort of forgets about the show and it becomes all about the hunt, which undercuts any sort of commentary the director may have been trying to make on him personally. The ending threw me for a bit, but Lucy and I worked it out to my satisfaction. The story ends with Dan, we see that character's full arc, but the Hansen story is just strangely dropped. That's because Hansen's story didn't end there, in real life, so he couldn't conclude the narrative arc with Hansen, it had to be with Dan. Through Dan we get to question was it all worth it? What was the price of your success? Hansen at that point in the story hadn't gotten to that point, and frankly never did.

Which brings me to something else that struck me about the movie — it was so stylized, so "movie like" (I actually thought to myself this was like a Neo-Hollywood movie) that I really never took it as an accurate depiction of Chris Hansen's life. It seemed obvious to me that this was a story using real characters and basic events, but not even attempting to lay claim to veracity. About the shittiest characterization of Hansen in the movie was the way he treated his kids, exposing them to all the garbage he deals with, only concerned that they see him as a success, when all they want is his attention. I actually thought that as I sat there in the theater with my two kids exposing them to all that garbage. Ironic, no?

After the movie Augie had a lot to share about how meaningful the movie was to him personally. He credits Hansen as one of the top three influences on him as a YouTuber and on the development and success not only of his show, but of the entire commentary community, which for better or worse gravitated towards Hansen's style and format. Keep in mind back then he was highly critical of Hansen, but he was one of the main guys bringing attention to Hansen in the commentary sphere. A lot of others picked up on it. But when you look at what was going on back then it's like everybody wanted to be Chris Hansen, it was always about exposing the naughty behavior of this or that YouTube star, and there seemed to be a neverending parade of such behavior, as was shown in the movie. People were confronted with leaked chats, lots of private salacious details exposed, people "canceled", ruined, etc. And like in the movie, these were very often bad people. But sometimes the mob turned on people unjustly, and that soured on him (Augie). He's since come around on Hansen as a man and his opinions of him are not as strident these days, but he hopes the movie makes people question mob justice. He related one story just recently where a guy was beaten on camera by a group of anti-pedo vigilantes because they caught him (18y male) with an underage girl (17y female). Predictably, it's gone beyond just cancelation these days.

Overall, well done, enjoyable, thought provoking movie that was not in fact a "let's be soft on pedos" opinion piece. At least, I didn't see it that way.
